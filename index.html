Source materials

Learn

The strongest material for each stage, free and paid. ★ Best pick marks the one to choose where a free and a paid option cover the same ground. Courses whose only certificate is for completion are here for what they teach, never for the certificate.

Bookshelf

Books

The books worth your time on top of the courses and docs, in the order you'll need them. ★ Essential books are the ones to own or finish. For each one you get the chapters to read, not the whole thing, so reading never pushes the build schedule back. Mark a book Done after its Day 30 review.

Workbench

Parts

What you own and what to buy, ranked in the order your projects need it. Both lists are yours to edit: add a part, change a quantity, fix a detail or delete it. When you buy something, press Bought and it moves into your inventory. Prices are rough estimates; check local shops first.

Skill-evaluated only

Credentials

Only certifications earned through a proctored exam or a graded project. For a research Masters, committees weigh research, projects and recommendation letters far above certificates, so this list is short on purpose.

Proof of work

Portfolio

Every finished project becomes evidence in four places: a GitHub repo, a short video, a LinkedIn post and a line on your CV. These steps are also in the tracker as PF items.

Just-in-time learning

Side skills

Mechanics, airflow, heat, power, PCB design, colour, typography, markets. You don't study these up front. Each one is tied to the project where you'll first need it, and you learn just enough to finish that project, inside its hours. They're also in the tracker as K items.

Your specialists

Community

Aerodynamicists, PCB designers, flight-controller developers and designers answer questions every day in these places. Join the few that match your current phase, read before you ask, and answer what you can: a visible history of helpful answers is part of your portfolio too.
